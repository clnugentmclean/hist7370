## Course Description
This class offers a brief introduction to digital methods, tools, and the critical debates that inform digital historical scholarship. It is designed to expose students to the wide variety of work being done computationally by historians and other humanities scholars. Each week, we will look at a different topic in the emergent field of digital history. We will spend the first half of class discussing readings and projects related to that week’s topic. During the second half of class, students will get a hands-on introduction to a particular tool or method, such as building an online public history exhibit using Collection Builder or building an interactive narrative map using Story Map JS. During the last several weeks of the course, students will choose a particular topic or method and develop their own expertise in that area through a small project.

Students do not need any prior experience in digital humanities or programming to take this class. The class is taught seminar-style and involves several hands-on activities. 


## Learning Goals
* Understand the field of digital humanities (and digital history) and be able to critically read and evaluate the work that is being produced in this field.
* Practice scholarly discussion and writing
* Deploy digital tools and methods in a small project
* Expand your digital literacies through hands-on engagement with digital tools and methods

Note: This course satisfies the introductory requirement for the [Graduate Certificate in Digital Humanities.](https://cssh.northeastern.edu/history/graduate/programs/graduate-certificate-in-digital-humanities/)
